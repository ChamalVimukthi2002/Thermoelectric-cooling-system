# Thermoelectric-cooling-system
Developing a thermoelectric cooling system focused on minimizing the time required to cool the cold side of a Peltier (TEC) module using Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) control.

The main engineering challenge is identifying the optimal control strategy over time while balancing three competing thermal effects: heat leakage into the insulated Styrofoam container, Joule heating generated by current flowing through the TEC module, and heat removal from the TEC's hot side. These effects interact and influence the achievable cooling rate and overall system performance.

The project explores the use of a dynamic system model and LQR-based control to optimize the transient cooling response, accounting for the thermal behaviour of both sides of the TEC and the effects of heat transfer to the surroundings.

**Key Engineering Objectives and Technical Work**
- Develop a mathematical model of the thermoelectric cooling system.
- Investigate the interaction between cold-side cooling, electrical power consumption, and hot-side heat dissipation.
- Analyze heat leakage into the insulated container and its effect on cooling performance over time.
- Apply Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) control to optimize the transient response and reduce the time required to reach the target cold-side temperature.
- Integrate temperature sensing and embedded control hardware for system monitoring and control.
- Evaluate the influence of operating conditions and thermal management on achievable cooling performance.

**Technologies and Concepts:** Thermoelectric cooling (TEC), Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR), thermal modelling, heat transfer, embedded control systems, temperature sensing, and control-system optimization.
